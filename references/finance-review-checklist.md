# Finance review checklist

Apply only relevant sections. Cite the exact contract location for every conclusion.

## 1. Parties, authority, and document integrity

- Legal names, registration identifiers, addresses, signing entities, and payment entities agree across documents.
- Signatory titles, powers of attorney, seals, and approval requirements are present or routed for verification.
- No material blanks, unresolved placeholders, broken definitions, missing annexes, or ambiguous precedence rules remain.
- Counterparty onboarding, sanctions, related-party, beneficial-owner, bank-account verification, and segregation-of-duties controls are completed when policy requires them.

## 2. Scope, quantity, price, and currency

- Goods/services, specifications, quantities, units, deliverables, dependencies, and change-order mechanics are measurable.
- Unit price × quantity, subtotals, discounts, tax, and grand total reconcile.
- The contract states whether prices are fixed, indexed, adjustable, capped, minimum-commitment, or take-or-pay.
- Currency, exchange-rate source/date, conversion responsibility, bank charges, withholding, and rounding are clear.
- Price schedules, quotes, bids, POs, SOWs, and approval documents agree.

## 3. Tax and invoicing

- Tax-inclusive/exclusive wording, tax category, invoicing entity, invoice type, invoice content, timing, and delivery method are stated.
- Tax amounts and rates are internally consistent. Treat the legal correctness of a rate or treatment as a tax-specialist question unless authoritative, current evidence is supplied.
- Withholding, gross-up, reverse-charge, permanent-establishment, customs/duties, and tax-change allocation are addressed when relevant.
- Payment is not made contingent on an impossible invoice condition, and invoicing is not triggered before the underlying obligation is sufficiently defined.

## 4. Payment, cash, and credit exposure

- Trigger, due date, calendar/business-day convention, payment method, bank account, currency, and supporting evidence are unambiguous.
- Milestone percentages total 100% and amounts reconcile to the applicable total.
- Advance payments have appropriate security, refund rights, milestones, or other controls.
- Deposits, retainage, set-off, deductions, disputed invoices, late fees, interest, credits, and refunds are bounded and operationally workable.
- Bank-detail changes require independent verification through an approved channel; contract text alone is not sufficient evidence to change master data.
- Credit terms and exposure fit approved limits or are escalated.

## 5. Delivery, acceptance, and revenue/cost triggers

- Delivery and acceptance criteria are objective, testable, timed, and assigned to an owner.
- Deemed acceptance, partial acceptance, rejection, cure, re-performance, and dependency delays are clear.
- Payment and invoicing triggers align with delivery/acceptance evidence.
- Identify terms that may affect accounting, including variable consideration, rights of return, rebates, options, financing components, principal-agent indicators, warranties, modifications, and multiple deliverables. Route final accounting conclusions to the accounting policy owner.

## 6. Discounts, rebates, credits, and price protection

- Thresholds, measurement periods, eligible spend/revenue, exclusions, true-up method, claim deadline, evidence, and settlement method are defined.
- Volume tiers and retroactive/prospective mechanics are mathematically testable.
- Most-favored pricing, benchmarking, inflation/indexation, and audit rights have clear data sources and timing.
- Rebates and credits are captured in the obligation register so benefits are not lost after signature.

## 7. Term, renewal, change, and termination

- Effective date, service commencement, initial term, renewal term, and renewal/termination notice windows are consistent.
- Auto-renewal, evergreen, minimum-spend, and early-termination charges are visible and owned.
- Termination rights, convenience rights, cure periods, transition assistance, data return, asset return, and final settlement are workable.
- Amendments, change orders, scope creep, and price changes require defined authority and documentary evidence.

## 8. Liability, remedies, and financial protection

- Liability caps, baskets, exclusions, super-caps, indemnities, service credits, liquidated damages, penalties, warranties, and insurance requirements are identified.
- Check whether caps are tied to fees paid, fees payable, annual fees, total contract value, or another base; calculate the exposure where possible.
- Remedies do not unintentionally duplicate or exclude each other, and service credits do not silently become the sole remedy unless accepted.
- Guarantees, letters of credit, parent guarantees, bonds, escrow, and security interests specify amount, issuer, validity, draw conditions, expiry, and release.
- Route enforceability and legal interpretation to counsel.

## 9. Data, audit, compliance, and records

- Record-retention, audit, access, reporting, data-security, confidentiality, data-location, subcontracting, and incident obligations have feasible owners and costs.
- Audit rights define scope, frequency, notice, cost allocation, confidentiality, and remediation.
- Regulatory or policy representations are not broader than the organization can evidence.

## 10. Post-signature operating controls

- Create tasks for every date- or event-driven obligation with owner, trigger, due date, recurrence, evidence, and escalation path.
- Capture committed spend/revenue, forecast timing, cash-flow dates, renewal exposure, rebate opportunities, price changes, guarantees, insurance expiries, and reporting duties.
- Store the executed version and all incorporated documents together; link amendments to the original agreement.
